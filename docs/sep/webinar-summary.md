# SEP Webinar Summary — Detailed Agent Training Guide

**Presenter:** Pam Harrison, Medicare Compliance Strategy & Education Lead, SPARC (Spark Advisors)
**Length:** ~55 minutes
**Purpose:** Deep dive into every Special Enrollment Period — when to use them, how to verify eligibility, and what gets agents flagged, suspended, or terminated.

---

## Why This Matters Right Now

MA OEP ends March 31. After that, SEPs are the **only way** to enroll clients until AEP opens again in October. Agents who don't understand SEPs are leaving money on the table. Agents who misuse them are getting terminated. Pam made it very clear: CMS and carriers are watching SEP usage "like I have never seen before in my time in the business."

---

## Quick Reference: Standard Enrollment Periods

Before SEPs, know these cold:

| Period | When | Who | Key Detail |
|--------|------|-----|------------|
| **IEP** (Initial) | 7-month window around turning 65 | New to Medicare | If they had Medicare under 65 (disability), they get a **second full IEP** at 65 |
| **GEP** (General) | Jan 1 – Mar 31 yearly | Missed their IEP | Late Enrollment Penalty applies. Coverage starts 1st of month after signup |
| **AEP** (Annual) | Oct 15 – Dec 7 | Everyone | Changes effective Jan 1 |
| **MA OEP** | Jan 1 – Mar 31 | Already on MA plan | One plan change. Effective 1st of following month |
| **New-to-MA OEP** | 3 months after IEP enrollment | First-time MA enrollees from IEP | One-time correction if they picked wrong plan during IEP |
| **Med Supp OEP** | 6 months after Part B + turning 65 | New Part B enrollees | **Guaranteed issue** — no underwriting. After this window, health underwriting applies |

## The SEPs — What Pam Actually Said

### SEP #1: Loss of Employer Coverage (MOST COMMON)

**Who it's for:** People who worked past 65, stayed on employer health plan, enrolled in Part A (free, extra hospital coverage), delayed Part B (didn't want to pay premium while employed).

**The two separate SEPs:**
- **8-month Medicare SEP** to sign up for Part B (via ssa.gov, by phone, or at SSA office)
- **2-month SEP** to enroll in a Medicare plan (MA or PDP) after group coverage ends

**What Pam said agents should do:** When a client calls and says "I'm retiring April 30th," walk them through:
1. Go to ssa.gov, sign up for Part B to start May 1
2. Enroll in a Medicare plan also starting May 1
3. Everything coincides with the employer coverage end date — **no gap in coverage**

**Pam's exact point:** "That's what you want to advise to your clients — that they want everything to coincide with that end of coverage date with their employer coverage so that they do not have any kind of a gap in coverage."

**Forms:** Delayed Part B enrollment likely requires L-565 and 40B forms (submitted at ssa.gov or SSA office).

**Why this is the most common:** More people working past 65 than ever before.

---

### SEP #2: Change of Residence (Moving)

**Who it's for:** Beneficiary moves out of their plan's service area (different county).

**Critical detail most agents miss:** Even if their current plan IS available at the new location, they can still switch if there are **new plan options** available there. Pam's example: They had 20 plans in their old county, move to a county with 25 plans — even if their plan is still available, they can change because new options exist.

**Timeframe — depends on when they notify the plan:**
- **Notify BEFORE moving:** SEP begins the month before the move + 2 full months after
- **Notify AFTER moving (or don't notify):** SEP begins the month they tell the plan + 2 full months after

**Other "moving" situations that fall under this SEP:**
- **Moving out of a nursing home/institution:** 2-month SEP after discharge (rehab, hip replacement, etc.)
- **Long-term institutional residents:** Continuous SEP (I-SNP plans)
- **Returning to the US after living abroad:** 2-month SEP. Must have Part A & B.
- **Released from incarceration:** 2-month SEP. Must have Part A & B (if they didn't keep paying Part B while incarcerated, they need to re-enroll first).

**Compliance warning from Pam:** CMS and carriers are checking with Social Security to verify address changes in the last 2-3 months. "I have seen this on allegations where the carrier comes back and says there has been no change to address in the last two or three months." They have to have **actually moved.**

### SEP #3: LIS / Extra Help Changes — TWO DIFFERENT SEPs

**This trips up agents.** There are two completely different SEPs here:

#### SEP 3A: Gaining or Losing Extra Help
- **Trigger:** Beneficiary just got approved for Extra Help, OR just lost eligibility (income went up)
- **Window:** 3 months to change MAPD or PDP plan
- **Effective date:** 1st of month following enrollment
- **Note:** Letters from Medicare/SSA can be delayed. The 3-month window accounts for this.

#### SEP 3B: Ongoing Monthly SEP for Existing LIS Recipients (NO CHANGE IN STATUS)
- **Who:** People who already have Extra Help — nothing changed, they just have it
- **What they can do:** Change to a different **PDP only** — once per month
- **What they CANNOT do:** Switch to MAPD or make any other plan type change
- **When this comes up:** Client on a PDP gets a new prescription that isn't covered. They can switch PDP plans.

**Pam's key point:** "If you've been in Medicare for a few years, you know that this is different than what it used to be." The rules changed — the ongoing monthly SEP is now PDP-only.

---

### SEP #4: Medicaid / Dual-Eligible — TWO DIFFERENT SEPs

Again, two separate SEPs that agents confuse:

#### SEP 4A: Change in Medicaid Status
- **Trigger:** Gaining, losing, or having a change in Medicaid level (QMB to SLMB, losing Medicaid but keeping LIS, etc.)
- **Applies to:** Both partial and full dual-eligible beneficiaries
- **Window:** 3 months to change MA or PDP plan
- **Effective date:** 1st of month following enrollment

#### SEP 4B: Ongoing Monthly Integrated SEP for Full Dual-Eligible
- **Who:** Full-benefit dual-eligible only (QMB Plus, SLMB Plus)
- **What they can do:** Enroll in an **integrated D-SNP** plan that aligns their Medicare and Medicaid coverage — once per month
- **THE BIG RULE:** The D-SNP carrier MUST MATCH the beneficiary's Medicaid MCO (Managed Care Organization)

**Pam's example:** Beneficiary has United for Medicaid (their MCO) but has a Humana Medicare Advantage plan. Those aren't integrated. They can use this monthly SEP to switch to a United D-SNP so both Medicare and Medicaid are under the same carrier.

**Eligible plan types for this SEP:**
- **FIDE** — Fully Integrated Dual Eligible SNP (most integrated)
- **HIDE** — Highly Integrated Dual Eligible SNP (less integrated, still qualifies)
- **AIP** — Applicable Integrated Plan

**Not all D-SNPs qualify as integrated.** Agents must check carrier portals to verify.

**How to find a beneficiary's MCO:** There is NO reliable automated way. Sunfire often returns "MCO info not found." The beneficiary needs to check their Medicaid card or call their state Medicaid office. Pam's advice: "If you're going to do a face-to-face appointment, do that when you're sitting with them and have them call."

**Q&A confirmed:** For both HIDE and AIP plans, the MCO MUST match the carrier. No exceptions.

**Do NOT enroll Medicaid beneficiaries in "give-back" plans.** If Medicaid is already paying their Part B premium, the give-back benefit provides zero value. Pam was direct: "You do not want to enroll them in a give-back plan."

---

### SEP #5: C-SNP (Chronic Condition SNP) — THE BIG OPPORTUNITY

**This is the one Pam was most excited about. Year-round SEP.**

**The numbers:** "There's like **17 million Medicare beneficiaries** who technically would qualify for a C-SNP plan because of a chronic condition that they have." A broker manager told Pam there were **twice as many people eligible for C-SNPs as D-SNPs** in her state.

**How it works:**
- Beneficiary has been diagnosed with a qualifying chronic condition
- A C-SNP plan covering that condition exists in their area
- They are NOT currently on a C-SNP for that condition
- They can enroll **any time of year** — year-round SEP

**No time limit from diagnosis.** Pam confirmed in Q&A: "As long as they have not had or are not currently on or have not been on a C-SNP for that condition in the past, there isn't a time limit." Diagnosed 5 years ago? Still eligible.

**Multiple conditions = multiple SEPs:** If a beneficiary is on a C-SNP for diabetes and gets diagnosed with COPD (and there's a C-SNP covering COPD in their area), they get a NEW SEP for the new condition.

**If they lose C-SNP eligibility** (no longer meet chronic condition criteria): 2-month SEP to join a regular (non-C-SNP) plan.

**Pam's message to agents:** "I believe part of our job as agencies is to educate clients on what is available. It might not always be the best fit, but we do have a responsibility to educate our clients." And: "This is a huge market and a great opportunity for you as agents."

**Bottom line:** If you're not marketing C-SNPs, you're ignoring the biggest year-round enrollment opportunity in Medicare.

---

### SEP #6: Natural Disaster

**When CMS declares a natural disaster** (hurricanes, floods, tornadoes), they may issue an SEP for the affected area. CMS determines the timeframe (usually long windows).

**THE RULE MOST AGENTS GET WRONG:** The beneficiary must have had **another election period that they missed** because of the disaster. This is NOT a standalone enrollment opportunity.

**Pam's example:** "If they were turning 65 and they were in their IEP, and tornado came through and took out their house — I doubt their Medicare was the top of their mind. So they didn't make a selection. That is an election period they missed due to the natural disaster."

**Cannot be advertised or marketed.** "You can't put out a blast saying we've had a tornado here in this area, you may be eligible to switch your plan due to that."

---

### SEP #7: Trial Right (Medigap/Supplement)

**Who it's for:** Beneficiary who dropped a Medicare Supplement to join Medicare Advantage **for the first time.**

**Window:** 12 months from the MA plan start date.

**What they can do:** Drop the MA plan, return to Original Medicare + Medicare Supplement + PDP with **guaranteed issue** on the supplement — no health underwriting.

**Key qualifier:** Must be the FIRST TIME they dropped a supplement for MA. Pam: "If it is the first time they have dropped their Medicare supplement to go to a Medicare Advantage plan, for the next 12 months they have a trial right."

**Why agents need to know this:** If you moved clients from supplement to MA last AEP (started Jan 1, 2026), they have all of 2026 to come back and say "I don't like this plan, I want my supplement back" — and you need to know that's available to them.

---

### SEP #8: Five-Star Plan

- **Window:** December 8 – November 30 (basically year-round, starting right after AEP)
- **One-time annual use**
- **What:** Switch from any plan to a five-star rated plan in their area
- **Current star rating doesn't matter** — even if they're on a 4.5 star plan, they can switch to a 5-star plan

---

### SEP #9: State Pharmaceutical Assistance Program (SPAP)

- **While enrolled in SPAP:** Can join MA or PDP plan **once per calendar year**, any time
- **If they lose SPAP eligibility:** 2-month SEP to make a plan change

**Verification required:** Beneficiary should have a card from the SPAP, or can call the state program directly. Sunfire does NOT have this info.

**Pam's warning:** This one "gets misused quite a bit." CMS and carriers verify actual enrollment and actual loss of eligibility.

---

### SEP #10: Accessible Format (ACC)

- For beneficiaries who need accessible format materials (braille, large print, different language)
- Only use when the beneficiary **genuinely** needs these materials

**Pam's reaction:** "This one is very crazy to me because most beneficiaries do not know that this exists." Translation: if an agent is using this at high volume, it's a red flag.

## Compliance — What Gets Agents Fired

Pam spent significant time on this. Key quote: "Agents are getting flagged, they're getting allegations, they're getting suspensions over misuse of SEP up to agent termination."

### The Election Period Verification Rule

**Before EVERY SEP enrollment, you MUST:**
1. Have a documented conversation with the beneficiary
2. Ask specific questions to verify they actually qualify
3. Explain the SEP you're using in a way the beneficiary understands
4. Get their affirmation that they understand

**On recorded telephonic enrollments:** The SEP verification conversation MUST be on the recording. "If they did not have a conversation with the client about asking the questions, discussing and verifying that election period with the client prior to enrollment on the recorded call, then they are getting dinged for that. They are getting a founded allegation."

**You cannot:** Assume, infer, or imply an SEP. You have to verify it.

### The Five Most Watched SEPs (Carrier Benchmarks)

| SEP | Must Be Less Than | What They Check |
|-----|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Weather/Disaster** | 15% of enrollments | Cannot advertise. Must have missed another election period. |
| **Moving** | 10% of enrollments | Social Security address change verification in last 2-3 months |
| **Loss of Creditable Coverage** | 4% of enrollments | Must be involuntary (plan terminated, not renewed) |
| **Accessible Format (ACC)** | 3% of enrollments | Most beneficiaries don't know this exists — high usage = red flag |
| **SPAP** | 5% of enrollments | CMS/carriers verify actual enrollment or actual loss of eligibility |

### What Happens When You Get It Wrong

If you submit an enrollment with the wrong SEP and the carrier or CMS kicks it back:
- You have to go back to the client
- Explain that the enrollment didn't go through
- Possibly redo the application
- "It just doesn't make for a good customer experience"

**Pam's advice:** "It's always better for you to ask a question about the SEP before you turn in the enrollment rather than turning in the enrollment incorrectly."

**When in doubt:** Reach out to your upline or compliance@sparkadvisors.com BEFORE submitting.

## The Teachable Takeaways

1. **C-SNPs are the biggest year-round opportunity.** 17 million eligible beneficiaries. No time limit from diagnosis. Year-round SEP. If you're not prospecting for C-SNP-eligible clients, you're leaving the largest off-season market untouched.

2. **There are TWO SEPs for LIS and TWO for Medicaid.** Don't confuse the "change in status" SEP (3-month window) with the "ongoing monthly" SEP. They have different rules and different plan-type restrictions.

3. **The disaster SEP is NOT a free enrollment pass.** They had to have missed another election period because of the disaster. Agents are getting terminated over this.

4. **Document everything.** Election period verification is not optional. If it's not on the recording, you're getting a founded allegation.

5. **Know the benchmarks.** If more than 15% of your enrollments are disaster SEP, more than 10% are moving, more than 4% are loss of coverage, more than 3% are ACC, or more than 5% are SPAP — you're getting flagged.

6. **Never enroll a Medicaid beneficiary in a give-back plan.** Medicaid is already paying their Part B. The give-back does nothing.

7. **The trial right is 12 months of guaranteed issue.** First-time supplement-to-MA switchers can go back with no underwriting. Know this for your clients.

8. **When in doubt, ask BEFORE you submit.** Wrong SEP = rejected enrollment = bad client experience = compliance risk.
